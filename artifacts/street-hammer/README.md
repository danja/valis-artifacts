# Street Hammer

Hardcore techno at 170 bpm in G minor, 152 bars (3:38), inspired by Carl Orff's "Gassenhauer" ("street hit"). The
feel is taken from the original: a bright tuned-percussion figure that bounces round every beat under short phrases of a
tune. The material is new (the ostinato is the three notes of each chord, the tune is eight bars of original melody),
so no melody of Orff's is used. Here the figure is on a marimba-mode modal bar, the tune is on a hoover lead, and under
them are a gabber kick (a sine that falls in pitch and is clipped after its envelope, so the tail stays loud),
an offbeat distorted bass, a TR-909 for hats, clap and snare rolls, and risers. The drops land with a long sub boom.
Hardcore: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_(electronic_dance_music_genre)

Structure: intro 16 bars, build 8, **drop** 32, break 16, build 8, **drop** 32 (tune an octave up, rolling bass),
break 8, **drop** 16, outro 16. A build ends in a snare roll with a gap on the last beat, then the drop.

* `mixes/street-hammer-v1.flac`: the mix, made with `valis-song mix --arc`. Integrated loudness -10.5 LUFS
  (the target was -10; the true-peak ceiling of -1 dBTP set the gain), loudness range 14.8 LU. Section levels: intro
  -20.2, drops -11.9 / -10.5 / -10.0, breaks -25.0 / -23.5. Tempo measured at 170.1 bpm. **Measured, not heard.**
* `song.ttl`, `score.ttl`: the layout and the notes as data. Both are generated by `../../scripts/street-hammer/make.py`
  from its table of sections and plays; change the table and run it again.
* `midi/`: kick, kit, bass, xylo, lead, riser (written by `valis-song score`).
* `circuits/`: `gabber-kick`, `xylo`, `hoover`, `riser` (written by `make.py`), `909`, `hc-bass` (a copy of
  `psych-bass.ttl`) and `reverb-return` (copies of the Valis examples).
* `wavs/`: the stems (ignored by git; rebuild with `valis-song render song.ttl`).

Rebuild: `python3 ../../scripts/street-hammer/make.py`, then `valis-song score`, `render`, `stems`, `mix song.ttl --version vN --arc`.

Notes from making it:

* The 909 in the Valis examples gated every drum but the kick from `NoteGate.velocity`, so a hit under velocity 64 was silent. The
  first render had no closed hats for that reason, and the hats were raised to 72 to 78. The circuit is now fixed in the Valis
  repository (`circuits/909.ttl` here is the fixed copy; re-rendering the kit gives the same stem, to the last digit of its rms),
  so a quieter hat would sound now, but the drums play at one level whatever the velocity, except the toms.
* `valis-song stems` put a note exactly on a bar line into the bar before it when the bar was not a whole number of
  samples (170 bpm gives 67764.7). It reported bars as silent that were not. Fixed in the Valis repository with a test.
* The listening is still to do: the balance of the kick and the bass, the hoover against the marimba in the same range
  (the figure sits at G3 to G4, the tune at G4 to F6), and whether the drops hit.
