# Tide Table: lyrics and how to sing them

The song is about a tide table, the printed sheet of the times of high and low water that hangs by the door of a harbour house. Someone reads it
every day because the one they wait for left with a tide and will come back on one. The sea is the only thing in the song that is certain, and
it is certain because it comes back. Nothing in the music hurries.

No vocal has been recorded. `wavs/vocal-guide.wav` is a guide: one note for each syllable, played by the choir circuit, so a singer can hear
where each word falls and how high it sits. `guide/tide-table-v1-with-guide.flac` is the mix with the guide laid on top of it. Both are measured and
not heard. The notes and words are made by `scripts/tide-table/vocal-guide.py`, which also writes this file.

## Where the voice sings

The voice is silent for the first 24 bars, so the listener learns the cello's tune before words arrive. Bars are counted from 1, at 78 beats per
minute, four beats to a bar (a bar is about 3.1 seconds).

| Bars | Time | Section | The voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 to 24 | 0:00 | intro, tide | Silent. The cello plays the tune. |
| 25 to 40 | 1:13 | rise | Verse, eight lines. |
| 41 to 48 | 2:03 | memory | Bridge, four lines, very soft, over the harp alone. |
| 49 to 64 | 2:27 | flood | Chorus, four lines sung twice. The last line changes the second time. |
| 65 to 72 | 3:16 | ebb | Three lines, fading. Hold the last word. |

Each line takes two bars. Syllables are written apart with hyphens in the script and joined here.

## Range and key

The key is D minor. Every note of the guide sits between A3 and C5, so the tune fits most voices an octave up or down (a low voice sings it as written;
a high one moves it up an octave). The guide sings no note outside the scale of D natural minor.

## How to sing it

* **Sing it plain.** Close to speaking, no vibrato until the held note at the end of a line, and then only a little. This is a harbour at night, not a theatre.
* **Start each line late.** The guide starts half a beat into the first bar. Keep that small hesitation; it is like breath taken before reading aloud.
* **Lines ask and answer.** The first line of each pair leans up and the second settles down, so it sounds like a question and its reply. Do not push either.
* **Hold the last word of every line** for about two and a half beats, then let it go. In the chorus hold "tide", "away", "edge" and "day" longest.
* **Dynamics.** Verse: mezzo-piano, close to the microphone. Bridge: almost a whisper, and breathy. Chorus: mezzo-forte, the one place the voice opens. Ebb:
  quieter again, and let the last "come in" fade into the cello.
* **The chorus is the cello's tune.** On the first line of each chorus the guide starts where the cello starts, so the voice sings in unison with it.
  If you want a harmony, sing a third above on the held notes.
* **Breath.** Breathe in the half beat before each line. Never in the middle of one.
* **Double track the chorus** if you record it, one take left and one right, and keep the verse single.
