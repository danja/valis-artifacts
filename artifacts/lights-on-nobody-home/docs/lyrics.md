# Lights On, Nobody Home: lyrics

Written against the score in `scripts/lights-on-nobody-home/track.py`. 128 bpm, 92 bars, about 2:53. Bars are counted
from the start of the song, so a singer can find the line. The words contain mild and strong swearing.

The verses are bright mariachi in C major: cheerful, almost a singalong, about people who are not very bright.
The chorus jumps up a whole step into D Hungarian minor (D E F G# A Bb C#) and turns into a punk chant. The verses
are in English with a little Spanish; every phrase in them is ordinary slang that people say.

The notes to sing are in [vocal-guide.md](vocal-guide.md), one note per syllable, and in `midi/vocal-guide.mid`.
The words below are exactly the words of that guide. A line is two bars in the verses, one bar in the pre-chorus and
the chorus.

## Verse 1 (bars 9 to 24)

The lights are on but nobody's home,
The lift doesn't go to the top floor, mate,
He's a sandwich short of a picnic,
Not the sharpest tool in the shed,

Ay, que burro, que burro, que burro,
Doesn't know his left from his right,
Dim as a Toc H lamp, a bit slow on the draw,
Brain's gone off for a Burton, thick as a door.

## Pre-chorus (bars 25 to 28)

Is anyone in?
Is anyone in?
Knock, knock, knock on his head,
Nothing, nothing there.

## Chorus (bars 29 to 36)

One word to a beat. The words in capitals are shouted.

BLANK! Blank! Blank! Blank!
BLANK! Blank! Blank! Blank!
Nobody home! Nobody home!
BLANK! Blank! Blank! Blank!
BLANK! Blank! Blank! Blank!
Nobody home! Nobody home!
Lights on, lights on, lights on, ...
... nobody home!

## Verse 2 (bars 37 to 52)

He's a few cards short of a full deck,
Two fries short of a Happy Meal,
A lost cause, a muppet, a plonker,
All hat and no cattle, my friend,

Ay, que burro, que burro, que burro,
Yes, he's a pillock, a numpty, a drip,
Dumb as a box of rocks, thick as pig shit,
A complete and utter tit, and he's proud of it.

## Pre-chorus (bars 53 to 56)

Is anyone in?
Is anyone in?
Knock, knock, knock on his head,
Not a soul, nobody home.

## Chorus (bars 57 to 64)

As the first chorus.

## Bridge (bars 67 to 72)

The trumpets play the bridge, so the voice has a rest, and then three spoken lines, flat, in the rhythm of the guide
over the harp and the solo:

"I asked him a question." (bars 67 and 68)
"Waited a week." (bars 69 and 70)
"The lights were on." (bars 71 and 72)

## Final chorus (bars 73 to 88)

As the chorus, twice through, 16 bars. In the second eight bars the whole band can shout each BLANK.

## Outro (bars 89 to 92)

Back in the happy key, sung, with the trumpets:

Ay, que burro, que burro, que burro,
que burro, que burro...
que burro!
Ay!

The last bar is a single hit on the first beat, held, with a crash.

## Notes

The idioms are all in common use and are not taken from any song: "the lights are on but nobody's home",
"a sandwich short of a picnic", "the lift does not go to the top floor", "not the sharpest tool in the shed",
"dim as a Toc H lamp" (a British expression, from the lamp of the Toc H fellowship founded in 1915,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toc_H), "gone for a Burton" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_for_a_Burton),
"all hat and no cattle", "dumb as a box of rocks". "Que burro" is Spanish slang: a *burro* is a donkey, and the
phrase means "how stupid". Verse 2 includes some coarse expressions. They can be softened for a radio edit by
singing "thick as a plank" and "a complete and utter fool".
